# RootsMagic 11: Biography Best Practices

**Document Version:** 1.0
**Last Updated:** 2025-01-08
**Purpose:** Guidelines for generating quality biographical narratives from RootsMagic data

---

## Overview

This document provides best practices for AI agents generating biographical narratives from RootsMagic databases. Well-written biographies transform genealogical data into engaging, accurate, and properly sourced narratives suitable for family histories, academic journals, or personal memorialization.

### Biography Goals

1. **Accuracy** - Present facts correctly with appropriate qualifiers
2. **Readability** - Engage readers with clear, flowing narrative
3. **Credibility** - Cite sources appropriately
4. **Completeness** - Cover major life events and transitions
5. **Context** - Place person in historical and familial setting

---

## Standard Biography Structure

### Recommended Sections

```
1. Introduction (Birth & Identity)
2. Early Life & Family Background
3. Education
4. Career & Occupation
5. Marriage & Family Life
6. Military Service (if applicable)
7. Migration & Residence
8. Later Life & Achievements
9. Death & Legacy
10. Sources & Notes
```

**Length Guidelines:**
- **Short bio:** 250-500 words (2-3 paragraphs)
- **Standard bio:** 500-1500 words (5-8 paragraphs)
- **Comprehensive bio:** 1500+ words (10+ paragraphs, multiple sections)

## Section 1: Introduction (Birth & Identity)

### Purpose
Establish person's identity, birth information, and context.

### Essential Information
- Full name (including nicknames, alternate names)
- Birth date and place
- Parents' names
- Brief characterization or notable achievement

### Writing Pattern

**Full Introduction:**
```
[Name] was born on [date] in [place], the [nth] child of [Father's name]
and [Mother's name]. [He/She] [key identifying characteristic or achievement].
```

**Example:**
> John Dorsey Iams was born on December 30, 1921, in Tulsa, Oklahoma, the son of
> William Leonard Iams and Lucy Virginia Dorsey. He would become a distinguished
> career officer in the Central Intelligence Agency, serving during some of the
> most critical years of the Cold War.

### Handling Uncertainty

**Unknown birth date:**
> John Smith was born about 1850 in Maryland, based on census records that
> consistently report his age.

**Unknown birthplace:**
> John Smith was born in 1850, likely in Maryland where his family resided
> according to the 1860 census.

**Estimated dates:**
> Based on his reported age in the 1860 census, John Smith was born circa 1850.

## Section 2: Early Life & Family Background

### Purpose
Provide context about childhood, family circumstances, and formative years.

### Essential Information
- Family composition (siblings)
- Socioeconomic status (if known)
- Geographic location
- Significant childhood events
- Religious/cultural background

### Writing Pattern

```
[Name] grew up in [place] as [one of X children/the youngest/etc.]. The family
[description of circumstances]. During [his/her] childhood, [significant events].
```

**Example:**
> John grew up in Tulsa during the oil boom of the 1920s. The second of three
> children, he attended local public schools and developed an early interest in
> current events and international affairs.

### Including Family Context

**Siblings:**
> John had two siblings: an older brother, William Jr. (born 1919), and a younger
> sister, Mary (born 1924).

**Family occupation:**
> His father worked as a petroleum engineer for one of Tulsa's major oil companies,
> providing the family with a comfortable middle-class lifestyle.

**Family tragedy:**
> The family faced hardship in 1930 when John's father lost his position during
> the early Depression years, forcing them to move to a smaller home.

## Section 3: Education

### Purpose
Document formal education and intellectual development.

### Essential Information
- Schools attended
- Degrees earned
- Graduation dates
- Academic achievements
- Educational interruptions (war, family needs)

### Writing Pattern

```
[Name] attended [school name] in [location], graduating in [year]. [He/She]
subsequently enrolled at [institution] where [he/she] studied [subject].
```

**Example:**
> John attended Central High School in Tulsa, graduating in 1938. He enrolled at
> the University of Oklahoma, where he majored in political science and participated
> in the debate team.

### Handling Incomplete Education

**Interrupted education:**
> John's college education was interrupted in 1943 when he enlisted in the U.S.
> Navy during World War II.

**Self-educated:**
> Though formal schooling ended at age fourteen due to family financial constraints,
> John was an avid reader and largely self-educated in history and literature.

---

## Section 4: Career & Occupation

### Purpose
Chronicle professional life and accomplishments.

### Essential Information
- Job titles and employers
- Career progression
- Notable achievements
- Career changes
- Retirement

### Writing Pattern

```
[Name] began [his/her] career as [position] with [employer] in [year]. Over the
next [X years], [he/she] [career progression]. [Notable achievements].
```

**Example:**
> Following his military service, John joined the Central Intelligence Agency in 1947
> as a junior analyst. Over his twenty-six-year career, he rose through the ranks,
> serving in Athens, Prague, and Langley, Virginia. He specialized in Eastern European
> affairs during the height of the Cold War.

### Multiple Occupations

**Career change:**
> Initially trained as a teacher, Mary taught elementary school for five years before
> transitioning to nursing in 1935, a profession she would practice for three decades.

**Concurrent occupations:**
> While operating his farm, John also served as the township's tax assessor from
> 1895 to 1910.

---

## Section 5: Marriage & Family Life

### Purpose
Document family formation and relationships.

### Essential Information
- Spouse name(s)
- Marriage date(s) and place(s)
- Children (names, birth years)
- Divorces or remarriages
- Family dynamics (if known)

### Writing Pattern

```
On [date], [Name] married [Spouse name] in [place]. [Description of spouse].
The couple had [number] children: [names and years].
```

**Example:**
> On June 15, 1947, John married Eleanor Ruth Davis of Boston, Massachusetts,
> whom he had met during his Navy service. Eleanor, a librarian and translator,
> would accompany John on his overseas postings. The couple had three children:
> David (born 1949), Susan (born 1952), and Thomas (born 1955).

### Multiple Marriages

**Divorce and remarriage:**
> John's first marriage to Mary Smith ended in divorce in 1885. Three years later,
> he married Sarah Johnson, with whom he spent the remainder of his life.

**Widowhood and remarriage:**
> Following the death of his first wife Elizabeth in 1892, John married her younger
> sister Margaret in 1894, a practice known as the levirate and not uncommon in
> the period.

### Childless Marriages

> Though their marriage remained childless, John and Mary were devoted to their
> nieces and nephews, often hosting them during summer months.

## Section 6: Military Service

### Purpose
Document military service and wartime experiences.

### Essential Information
- Branch of service
- Rank(s)
- Service dates
- Units/assignments
- Theaters of operation
- Awards/decorations
- Discharge status

### Writing Pattern

```
[Name] served in the [Branch] from [year] to [year], achieving the rank of
[rank]. [He/She] was stationed [locations] and participated in [campaigns/operations].
```

**Example:**
> John enlisted in the U.S. Navy in April 1943, serving aboard the destroyer
> USS Fletcher in the Pacific Theater. He participated in operations at Guadalcanal
> and Okinawa, earning the Bronze Star for valor. He was honorably discharged in
> October 1945 with the rank of Lieutenant Junior Grade.

### Draft Registration (non-service)

> John registered for the World War II draft on June 5, 1917, at age 35. As a
> father of four children, he was classified 4-F and did not serve in combat.

## Section 7: Migration & Residence

### Purpose
Document geographic movements and reasons for relocation.

### Essential Information
- Significant moves with dates
- Reasons for migration (if known)
- Census locations over time
- Final residence

### Writing Pattern

```
[Name] resided in [place] until [year], when [he/she] moved to [new place]
[reason if known]. [He/She] would remain in [area] for [duration/remainder of life].
```

**Example:**
> Born in Tulsa, John lived there until his CIA career took him abroad. He resided
> in Athens, Greece (1950-1953) and Prague, Czechoslovakia (1956-1964) before
> returning to the United States. He settled in Langley, Virginia, in 1964, where
> he remained until his retirement in 1973. He spent his final years in Santa Fe,
> New Mexico.

### Immigration

> The family emigrated from County Cork, Ireland, in 1849, fleeing the potato famine.
> After landing in New York, they traveled westward to settle in Pennsylvania's
> coal mining region.

## Section 8: Later Life & Achievements

### Purpose
Cover post-retirement years and late-life accomplishments.

### Essential Information
- Retirement date and location
- Post-career activities
- Community involvement
- Health issues (if relevant)
- Personal interests

### Writing Pattern

```
Following [his/her] retirement in [year], [Name] [activities]. [He/She] remained
active in [community involvement/hobbies/interests].
```

**Example:**
> After retiring from the CIA in 1973, John relocated to Santa Fe, New Mexico, where
> he pursued his lifelong interest in southwestern art and Native American culture.
> He volunteered at the local museum and wrote occasional articles for intelligence
> journals under his Agency pseudonym.

## Section 9: Death & Legacy

### Purpose
Document death and summarize lasting impact.

### Essential Information
- Death date and place
- Cause of death (if known and appropriate)
- Age at death
- Burial location
- Survivors
- Legacy/remembrance

### Writing Pattern

```
[Name] died on [date] in [place] at the age of [age] [cause if known and appropriate].
[He/She] was buried at [location]. [He/She] was survived by [survivors].
[Legacy statement].
```

**Example:**
> John Dorsey Iams died on June 2, 1996, in Santa Fe, New Mexico, at the age of 74.
> He was buried at the National Memorial Cemetery of Arizona. He was survived by his
> wife Eleanor, three children, and seven grandchildren. His career spanned a critical
> period in American intelligence history, and his contributions to Cold War operations
> remain partially classified.

### Uncertain Death Information

**Unknown death date:**
> John Smith likely died between 1892 and 1900, as he appears in the 1890 census
> but his wife is listed as a widow in 1900.

**Unknown burial:**
> Though the location of his burial is unknown, family tradition holds that he was
> interred in the family cemetery near their homestead.

1. Historical events during lifetime

## Handling Uncertainty

### Qualifying Language

**Certainty Levels:**

| Certainty | Language | Example |
|-----------|----------|---------|
| Certain | No qualifier | "John was born on June 15, 1850" |
| Probable | "likely", "probably" | "John was probably born in Maryland" |
| Possible | "possibly", "perhaps" | "John possibly served in the Civil War" |
| Estimated | "about", "circa", "approximately" | "John was born about 1850" |
| Calculated | "based on", "calculated from" | "Based on his age in the 1860 census, John was born circa 1850" |
| Unknown | "unknown", "not recorded" | "His birthplace is unknown" |

### Evidence Strength Indicators

**Strong evidence (multiple sources):**
> John Smith was born on June 15, 1850, in Baltimore, Maryland, according to his
> birth certificate, baptismal record, and death certificate.

**Moderate evidence (single source):**
> According to his tombstone, John Smith was born in 1850.

**Weak evidence (inference):**
> Based on his reported age of 10 in the 1860 census, John Smith was born
> approximately 1850.

**Conflicting evidence:**
> John's birth year is variously reported as 1849 (tombstone) and 1850 (census
> records). The 1850 date appears more reliable given its contemporary documentation.

## Privacy Considerations

### Living Persons Rule

**Guideline:** Do not publish detailed biographies of living persons without consent

**Privacy Period:** 110 years from birth (conservative standard)

**Exceptions:**
- Public figures with information in public domain
- Explicit consent obtained
- Biography for private family use only

### IsPrivate Flag Handling

```python
def should_include_event(event, person_birth_year, current_year=2025):
    """
    Determine if event should be included in biography.

    Args:
        event: Event dict with is_private flag
        person_birth_year: Year person was born
        current_year: Current year (default 2025)

    Returns:
        bool: True if event should be included
    """
    # Always exclude private events
    if event.get('is_private'):
        return False

    # Check if person is likely living
    if person_birth_year and (current_year - person_birth_year) < 110:
        # Person may be living - exclude sensitive events
        sensitive_types = ['Occupation', 'Residence', 'Education']
        if event['type_name'] in sensitive_types:
            return False

    return True
```

### Sensitive Information

**Exclude from public biographies:**
- Social Security Numbers
- Medical details (except historical context)
- Financial information (except property for historical context)
- Living persons' addresses
- DNA test results

## Source Citation Formatting

### In-Text Citation Styles

**Footnote Style (Academic):**
> John Smith was born on June 15, 1850, in Baltimore, Maryland.¹

**Parenthetical Style (Genealogical):**
> John Smith was born on June 15, 1850, in Baltimore, Maryland (Birth Certificate,
> Baltimore City Records).

**Narrative Style (Popular):**
> According to his birth certificate on file with Baltimore City, John Smith was
> born on June 15, 1850.

### End Notes Section

```
SOURCES

1. John Smith Birth Certificate, Baltimore City Vital Records, Certificate No. 1234,
   June 16, 1850.

2. U.S. Federal Census, 1860, Maryland, Baltimore City, Ward 3, p. 15, dwelling 112,
   family 115, John Smith.

3. Smith Family Bible Record, in possession of Mary Johnson, Baltimore, Maryland.
```

### Source Quality Indicators

```python
def describe_source_quality(proof_level, citation_count):
    """Describe source quality for a fact."""

    if citation_count == 0:
        return "unsourced"
    elif citation_count == 1:
        if proof_level == 1:
            return "documented"
        else:
            return "according to one source"
    else:  # Multiple sources
        if proof_level == 1:
            return "well-documented"
        elif proof_level == 3:
            return "documented but disputed"
        else:
            return "documented in multiple sources"
```

**Examples:**
- "John's birth date is well-documented in both vital records and church registers."
- "The marriage date, though documented in family records, cannot be confirmed by official sources."
- "While unsourced, family tradition holds that..."

```
[Photo credit/source].
```

**Example:**

## Tone and Style Guidelines

### Voice and Perspective

**Use third person throughout:**
- ✓ "John was born..."
- ✗ "He was born..." (start of biography)

**Maintain consistent past tense:**
- ✓ "John worked as a farmer."
- ✗ "John works as a farmer."

**Be objective but not clinical:**
- ✓ "John faced numerous hardships during his early life."
- ✗ "Subject experienced negative life events."

### Sentence Variety

**Vary sentence structure:**
```
[Simple] John was born in 1850.
[Compound] He attended local schools and worked on the family farm.
[Complex] Although he had limited formal education, John became a successful merchant.
```

### Paragraph Flow

**Use transitions:**
- Chronological: "Following", "Subsequently", "Later", "Eventually"
- Contrast: "However", "Despite", "Although", "Nevertheless"
- Addition: "Additionally", "Moreover", "Furthermore"
- Causation: "As a result", "Consequently", "Therefore"

**Example paragraph:**
> John completed his elementary education in 1864. However, the Civil War had
> devastated his family's finances. Consequently, rather than continuing his
> schooling, he began working full-time on the farm. Nevertheless, he remained
> an avid reader throughout his life.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### 1. Genealogical Jargon

**Avoid:**
- ✗ "John married Mary, d/o James Smith"
- ✗ "John b. 1850, d. 1920"
- ✗ "He m. Mary Smith on 15 Jun 1875"

**Use:**
- ✓ "John married Mary, the daughter of James Smith"
- ✓ "John was born in 1850 and died in 1920"
- ✓ "He married Mary Smith on June 15, 1875"

### 2. Data Dumping

**Avoid:**
> John lived at 123 Main Street (1880-1885), 456 Oak Avenue (1885-1890),
> 789 Pine Road (1890-1895)...

**Use:**
> John moved frequently during the 1880s and 1890s, residing at various
> addresses in Baltimore before finally settling on Pine Road in 1890.

### 3. Speculation Without Qualification

**Avoid:**
- ✗ "John must have been upset about..."
- ✗ "She probably felt..."
- ✗ "This indicates that he believed..."

**Use:**
- ✓ "John's subsequent actions suggest..."
- ✓ "The circumstances likely affected..."
- ✓ "This may indicate..."

### 4. Anachronistic Language

**Avoid modern terms for historical context:**
- ✗ "John relocated to maximize career opportunities"
- ✗ "Mary struggled with depression"

**Use period-appropriate language:**
- ✓ "John moved west seeking better prospects"
- ✓ "Mary suffered from melancholy"

### 5. Excessive Detail

**Avoid listing every event:**
- ✗ Every census record
- ✗ Every property transaction
- ✗ Every minor fact

**Synthesize information:**
> Census records from 1860 through 1900 consistently show John working as a
> farmer in Adams County, Pennsylvania.

## Python: Biography Generation Template

```python
def generate_biography(person_id, db_path, length='standard'):
    """
    Generate biographical narrative from RootsMagic data.

    Args:
        person_id: PersonID from database
        db_path: Path to RootsMagic database
        length: 'short', 'standard', or 'comprehensive'

    Returns:
        str: Formatted biography text
    """
    conn = connect_rmtree(db_path)

    # Get person data
    person = get_person_data(person_id, conn)

    # Get events
    events = get_person_events(person_id, conn, include_private=False)

    # Get family relationships
    family = get_family_relationships(person_id, conn)

    # Build biography sections
    bio_sections = []

    # Section 1: Introduction
    intro = generate_introduction(person, events)
    bio_sections.append(intro)

    # Section 2-8: Life stages based on available events
    if has_events_in_category(events, 'early_life'):
        bio_sections.append(generate_early_life(person, family, events))

    if has_events_in_category(events, 'education'):
        bio_sections.append(generate_education(person, events))

    if has_events_in_category(events, 'career'):
        bio_sections.append(generate_career(person, events))

    if has_events_in_category(events, 'family'):
        bio_sections.append(generate_family_life(person, family, events))

    if has_events_in_category(events, 'military'):
        bio_sections.append(generate_military_service(person, events))

    if has_events_in_category(events, 'migration'):
        bio_sections.append(generate_migration(person, events))

    # Section 9: Death & Legacy
    death = generate_death_legacy(person, family, events)
    bio_sections.append(death)

    # Combine sections
    biography = '\n\n'.join(bio_sections)

    # Add citations if standard/comprehensive
    if length in ['standard', 'comprehensive']:
        citations = generate_citations(person_id, conn)
        biography += f"\n\n{citations}"

    conn.close()
    return biography
```
